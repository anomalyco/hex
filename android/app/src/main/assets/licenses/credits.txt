The English, Spanish, German, French and Italian capitalization lists (.capitals) come from the same corpus's
word counts for 1980–2019: the share of each word written with a capital first letter.
The Spanish, German, French and Italian validity lists (.valid) come from the same word counts:
spellings outside each model used at least 3,000 times (German: 10,000), with each word's count
quantized to a cost.

German, French and Italian use their corpus's alphabetic spellings; no external dictionary. French and
Italian keep the books' elided forms ("c'est", "dell'anno") and leave out the same letters run together.
English literal validity also uses SCOWL words, proper names, uppercase forms and abbreviations
outside the frequency model, lowercased and sorted; these spellings carry no word probabilities.
