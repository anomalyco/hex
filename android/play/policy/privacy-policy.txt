Hex for Android
Last updated November 25, 2026

Anomaly Innovations, Inc. makes Hex and Hex Keyboard for Android. We do not collect or share your personal data, except feedback you choose to send. Hex has no accounts, ads, tracking or analytics.

Typing
In ordinary text fields, Hex uses nearby text and your taps to suggest words and correct spelling on your phone. It keeps no keystroke log. In fields that Android marks as passwords, including visible passwords and PINs, Hex types literally. It does not read the field's text, offer suggestions or start dictation.

Dictation
Hex uses the microphone only when you start dictating. It processes the audio in memory, without saving a recording, and turns speech into text on your device. Translation and cleanup also run on your device. Hex keeps no transcript history. The app where you insert text may save or send it according to its own policy.

Model downloads
During setup, Hex asks Hugging Face and its delivery network for one public speech model (190 MB) over HTTPS. If you turn on Faster English dictation in Labs and tap Download, or turn on Pick the English model for this phone in Labs and it finds English dictation slow on your phone, Hex asks them the same way for a second public model (135 MB) that it uses for English. The host sees the IP address needed to deliver each file. Hex does not send your words, audio, account information or device identifier with either request. After a download, no connection is needed to use the model. If the first is interrupted, you can retry or use the smaller model included in Hex.

Both models wait for Wi-Fi unless you tap Use mobile data. To know whether the phone's network charges for data, Hex has Android's network state permission (ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE). Hex uses it only to ask Android whether the network is metered, such as mobile data, or not, such as most Wi-Fi. Android leaves the network's name out of that answer, and the permission doesn't let Hex see what goes over the network. Android grants it when Hex is installed, without asking.

Feedback you send
Hex's Send feedback form, on Home, sends nothing until you tap Send. Then Hex sends, over HTTPS, to Anomaly's feedback inbox (Bellhop, run by Anomaly): your message, and a subject and kind if you chose them; the pictures shown in the form, which start with a picture of Hex's own screen taken when you opened the form (never the rest of your phone's screen, and with any dictation shown on Home painted over) and any photos you add, each removable before you send; Hex's version, your phone's model, its Android version and language, the screen you opened the form from, and the language and speech model Hex dictates with; and your email, only if you type one, so Anomaly can reply.

Nothing is emailed when you send. If Anomaly replies, you get an email and confirm your address once. With an email, Hex also sends a random install ID it makes the first time, so your reports stay together; it is not tied to your phone or Google account, and uninstalling Hex removes it. Without an email, feedback is anonymous: no email and no install ID, and it can't be answered. Feedback never includes your dictations, transcripts, recordings, vocabulary, corrections or anything you type in other apps, and the Hex keyboard never sends it. Anomaly uses feedback only to answer you and fix what you report. Like any request, the server sees your IP address.

What stays on your phone
Hex saves your settings, setup progress and downloaded models in private app storage; Delete download in Labs removes the second one. If you undo an autocorrection, Hex keeps a count against a hash of that word pair so it can avoid repeating the correction. It does not store the original words. If you turn on Learn from my fixes in Labs, Hex keeps the word pairs you corrected right after dictating, how often and when you last corrected each, on this phone only; it never sends them, and it learns nothing from password fields. Turning it off stops the learning but keeps the pairs until you tap Delete what Hex learned, which removes them. If you turn on Adaptive touch in Labs, Hex keeps, for each letter key, an average of where your taps land on it, in a table of fixed size (under 32 KB) on this phone only; it keeps no letters, words or order of taps, never sends it, and learns nothing from password, email or web address fields. Turning it off stops the learning but keeps the averages until you tap Reset touch learning or Delete what Hex learned, which remove them. If you turn on Pick the English model for this phone in Labs, Hex keeps, on this phone only, how long each of your last five counted English dictations took to transcribe against how long you spoke, how many dictations in a row were in English, how many it didn't count and why, and the date it chose the faster model; it keeps no words or audio and never sends these numbers. Turning it off deletes them. If you turn on Clipboard history in Labs, Hex reads the text you copy while it is your keyboard and keeps the last 20 texts on this phone only, in storage left out of backups and transfers to a new phone; each is deleted an hour after you copy it unless you pin it. If Android has stopped Hex by then, the text is deleted the next time the keyboard starts, and if the phone is asleep, when its screen next comes on. It never keeps text an app marks as sensitive, such as a password manager's, text copied while a password field is open, text Hex copied itself, or images and files. It never sends them, and turning it off deletes them all. If you turn on SwiftKey words in Labs and import a file you choose, such as SwiftKey's data export, Hex keeps only the single words in it, up to 50,000, on this phone only, in storage left out of backups and transfers to a new phone; from SwiftKey's ZIP it reads only the word list. It never sends them. Turning it off stops suggesting them but keeps them until you tap Remove imported words, which deletes them. Clearing Hex's app storage or uninstalling Hex removes this data. Microphone access can be revoked in Android settings.
